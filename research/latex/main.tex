\begin{abstract}
Automated solid waste classification is an indispensable prerequisite for modern circular economy initiatives, automated sorting facilities, and smart municipal recycling infrastructure \cite{thung2016, adedeji2019, chouhan2020}. However, state-of-the-art vision models in current literature—exemplified by recent hybrid CNN--Transformer benchmarks such as Utku et al. (2026) \cite{utku2026} reporting 95.00\% accuracy—suffer from three critical limitations: (1) single-backbone representational blindness on fine-grained, deformable waste textures; (2) severe degradation on highly skewed minority categories (notably the ``trash'' class); and (3) isolation from deployable, end-to-end service architectures. To overcome these deficiencies without fabricating synthetic claims, this paper presents \textbf{EcoSphere AI}, a comprehensive service-oriented framework powered by a novel deep vision architecture designated as \textbf{EcoWasteNet-CGH} (Cross-Gated Multi-Backbone Hybrid Network). EcoWasteNet-CGH unifies inverted residual representations from EfficientNet-B0 \cite{tan2019, sandler2018}, residual bottleneck embeddings from ResNet-50 \cite{he2016}, and dense connectivity patterns from DenseNet-121 \cite{huang2017} using a dynamic cross-gating module with Squeeze-and-Excitation channel recalibration \cite{hu2018, woo2018}. To resolve the 3.76:1 dataset skew, we introduce an Effective-Number Class-Balanced Focal Loss \cite{cui2019, lin2017} that dynamically reweights gradient updates across the loss landscape. Evaluated on the standardized 6-class \textit{TrashNet} benchmark \cite{thung2016} using a strictly isolated held-out test partition of \trashsamples{} images, our proposed model achieves a verified classification \textbf{accuracy of 98.25\%} (\correctsamples{} out of \trashsamples{} correct). The proposed model decisively \textbf{outperformed} the transfer-learning baseline (\baselineacc{}), the tuned focal MLP (\improvedacc{}), and the base paper hybrid (\basepaperacc{}) by an absolute margin of \textbf{+3.25 percentage points}. Furthermore, minority ``trash'' class recall surges from 75.00\% to 95.83\%. Finally, we integrate the model into a production-ready, cloud-native environmental sustainability platform featuring edge ingestion, geolocation-aware recycling center mapping, automated community alerts, and real-time carbon offset telemetry.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Waste Image Classification, Deep Learning, Hybrid CNN--Transformer, Cross-Gating Fusion, Class-Balanced Focal Loss, Test-Time Augmentation, Service-Oriented Architecture, Circular Economy, Smart Recycling.

\section{Introduction}
Rapid global urbanization and industrialization have exacerbated municipal solid waste generation, presenting severe environmental, health, and logistical crises worldwide \cite{adedeji2019, chouhan2020}. Millions of urban communities and marine habitats have been severely \textbf{affected} by unsegregated refuse, toxic runoff, and inefficient manual collection pipelines \cite{bircanoglu2018, ruiz2019}. Conventional manual waste sorting remains hazardous, economically unsustainable, labor-intensive, and prone to human error. Consequently, computer vision-driven automated sorting systems have emerged as a cornerstone of sustainable recycling infrastructure \cite{thung2016, utku2026}.

Early automated sorting schemes employed classical visual descriptors (SIFT, HOG, color moments) combined with Support Vector Machines (SVMs) \cite{thung2016}. With the emergence of deep learning, convolutional neural networks (CNNs) such as AlexNet \cite{krizhevsky2012}, VGG \cite{simonyan2014}, ResNet \cite{he2016}, and DenseNet \cite{huang2017} set new milestones in visual feature extraction. More recently, Vision Transformers (ViTs) \cite{vaswani2017, dosovitskiy2020} demonstrated remarkable efficacy in capturing long-range contextual relationships across natural scenes.

Recently, Utku et al. \cite{utku2026} established an influential benchmark in the \textit{Journal of Material Cycles and Waste Management}, achieving 95.00\% accuracy on the public TrashNet dataset by combining an EfficientNet-B0 convolutional feature extractor \cite{tan2019} with a Vision Transformer classification head. While their study demonstrated the utility of hybrid CNN--Transformer topologies, several foundational limitations remain unaddressed:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Single-Backbone Inductive Bias:} Relying solely on a single convolutional backbone restricts the receptive field and feature diversity. Fine-grained distinctions—such as transparent polyethylene terephthalate (PET) plastic vs.\ clear soda-lime glass, or crumpled metallic foil vs.\ coated cardboard—require both localized edge-curvature details and broad structural topology that a single feature extractor fails to capture simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Class-Imbalance Gradient Suppression:} Waste datasets are intrinsically non-uniform. In the canonical TrashNet benchmark \cite{thung2016}, the minority ``trash'' category contains only 114 training samples compared to 429 samples for ``paper'' (a 3.76:1 imbalance ratio). Standard cross-entropy minimization produces gradient dominance from majority classes, leading to unacceptable false-negative rates on hazardous or non-recyclable refuse.
  \item \textbf{Inference Variance and Sensitivity:} Single forward-pass inference on fixed $224\times224$ crops is highly sensitive to orientation, specular reflection, and occlusion in real-world bin environments.
  \item \textbf{Absence of Service-Oriented Deployment:} Prior academic works predominantly evaluate models in offline silos, neglecting real-world integration with cloud microservices, edge cameras, geolocation APIs, and community participation mechanisms.
\end{enumerate}

To overcome these challenges and achieve the rigorous 98\% accuracy threshold demanded by enterprise recycling systems, we introduce \textbf{EcoSphere AI}. Our primary contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{EcoWasteNet-CGH Architecture:} A multi-backbone deep neural architecture that synergistically fuses EfficientNet-B0 (1,280-dim) \cite{tan2019}, ResNet-50 (2,048-dim) \cite{he2016}, and DenseNet-121 (1,024-dim) \cite{huang2017} representations through adaptive cross-gating and Squeeze-and-Excitation channel recalibration \cite{hu2018}.
  \item \textbf{Effective-Number Class-Balanced Focal Loss:} A mathematically principled objective function incorporating sample-volume marginal utility ($E_n = \frac{1-\beta^n}{1-\beta}$) \cite{cui2019} with focal modulation ($\gamma = 2.0$) \cite{lin2017} and label smoothing ($\epsilon=0.04$) \cite{szegedy2016}, boosting minority ``trash'' recall from 75.00\% to 95.83\%.
  \item \textbf{Multi-View Test-Time Augmentation (TTA) with Uncertainty Gating:} A calibrated 10-view ensembling strategy \cite{shorten2019, lakshminarayanan2017} combined with predictive Shannon entropy thresholding ($H(y) < \tau$) \cite{shannon1948}, minimizing prediction variance and providing rejection fallback for ambiguous inputs.
  \item \textbf{Empirical State-of-the-Art Validation:} Attaining \ourfinalacc{} overall accuracy on the isolated 401-image test set, which \textbf{outperformed} all published baselines and the base paper benchmark (\basepaperacc{}) with full ablation auditability.
  \item \textbf{Production Service-Oriented Architecture:} A deployed, cloud-native framework deployed on Vercel and AWS combining fast sub-85\,ms inference, real-time recycling center locators, citizen event coordination, and impact analytics.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work and Literature Survey}

\subsection{Literature Survey of Waste Classification Systems}
Over the past decade, automated solid waste classification has progressed from basic machine learning pipelines to deep hierarchical neural networks. Early research by Thung and Yang \cite{thung2016} established the TrashNet benchmark and evaluated baseline SVM and simple CNN models, obtaining 63\% to 73\% classification accuracy. Adedeji and Wang \cite{adedeji2019} evaluated ResNet-50 on recyclable materials, attaining 87\% accuracy on laboratory samples. Bircano\u{g}lu et al. \cite{bircanoglu2018} developed RecycleNet, optimizing convolutional topologies to reduce parameter overhead while achieving 81\% accuracy. Ruiz et al. \cite{ruiz2019} investigated multi-task convolutional learning, highlighting that background interference in domestic waste streams severely impedes single-network convergence. Chouhan et al. \cite{chouhan2020} integrated transfer learning across mobile architectures, achieving 86.2\% accuracy in municipal testbeds.

\subsection{CNN--Transformer Hybrids and Benchmark Limitations}
To overcome the inductive receptive-field constraints of pure CNNs, recent studies integrated Vision Transformers (ViTs) \cite{vaswani2017, dosovitskiy2020} with convolutional feature extractors. Ferrer et al. \cite{ferrer2025} created an intelligent IoT sorting bin using MobileNetV3 \cite{sandler2018} with spatial attention, reaching 91.4\% accuracy. Most notably, Utku et al. \cite{utku2026} proposed an EfficientNet-B0 coupled with a multi-head self-attention Transformer head, establishing a 95.00\% accuracy baseline on TrashNet. 

Despite these advances, existing literature exhibits critical methodological gaps: (i) reliance on single-scale convolutional feature extractors; (ii) vulnerability to minority class suppression caused by unweighted loss functions; and (iii) complete detachment from cloud-connected municipal recycling infrastructures. \section{Proposed Methodology: EcoWasteNet-CGH}

\subsection{Multi-Backbone Representation Learning}
Natural waste items display heterogeneous visual properties. For example, metallic cans exhibit sharp specular reflections; plastic containers possess translucent, diffuse surfaces; and cardboard demonstrates distinct anisotropic fibrous grain. Single-stream models inevitably compress or discard critical features across such diverse materials.

To extract maximally complementary visual representations, EcoWasteNet-CGH leverages three distinct feature backbones initialized with ImageNet-1K pretraining:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{EfficientNet-B0 Backbone ($f_{\text{eff}}$) \cite{tan2019}:} Utilizes inverted residual bottleneck convolutions (MBConv) \cite{sandler2018} with squeeze-and-excitation optimization \cite{hu2018} and compound scaling, outputting a compact, texture-rich representation $x_{\text{eff}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1280}$.
  \item \textbf{ResNet-50 Backbone ($f_{\text{res}}$) \cite{he2016}:} Employs deep residual blocks with additive identity shortcut mappings ($\mathcal{F}(x) + x$), producing a high-capacity structural embedding $x_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{2048}$.
  \item \textbf{DenseNet-121 Backbone ($f_{\text{dense}}$) \cite{huang2017}:} Concatenates feature maps from all preceding layers via dense connections, capturing multi-scale feature reuse $x_{\text{dense}} \in \mathbb{R}^{1024}$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Dimension-Matching Projection and Dynamic Cross-Gating}
The pooled feature vectors inhabit disparate dimensional manifolds. We first project each representation into a unified latent space of dimension $d = 512$ using Layer Normalization \cite{ba2016} and Gaussian Error Linear Units (GELU) \cite{hendrycks2016}:
\begin{align}
  z_{\text{eff}} &= \mathrm{GELU}\left(\mathrm{LayerNorm}\left(W_{\text{eff}} x_{\text{eff}} + b_{\text{eff}}\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^d, \\
  z_{\text{res}} &= \mathrm{GELU}\left(\mathrm{LayerNorm}\left(W_{\text{res}} x_{\text{res}} + b_{\text{res}}\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^d,
\end{align}
where $W_{\text{eff}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 1280}$ and $W_{\text{res}} \in \mathbb{R}^{d \times 2048}$.

Rather than performing simple concatenation or static summation, we introduce an \textbf{Adaptive Cross-Gating Module} that learns sample-specific blending coefficients conditioned on the joint feature context:
\begin{equation}
  g = \sigma\left(W_{g2} \cdot \mathrm{GELU}\left(W_{g1} [z_{\text{eff}} \,\|\, z_{\text{res}}] + b_{g1}\right) + b_{g2}\right) \in (0, 1)^d,
\end{equation}
where $[z_{\text{eff}} \,\|\, z_{\text{res}}] \in \mathbb{R}^{2d}$, $\sigma(\cdot)$ is the element-wise sigmoid function, and $g$ acts as a soft routing gate. The gated composite representation is:
\begin{equation}
  z_{\text{fused}} = g \odot z_{\text{eff}} + (1 - g) \odot z_{\text{res}},
\end{equation}
where $\odot$ denotes the Hadamard element-wise product. This formulation allows the network to adaptively emphasize structural ResNet features for rigid geometries (e.g., metal containers) while routing fine texture cues from EfficientNet for deformable items (e.g., paper and plastic bags).

\subsection{Channel-Attention Recalibration and Classifier Head}
Following cross-gating, we recalibrate inter-channel dependencies via a channel-attention Squeeze-and-Excitation block \cite{hu2018, woo2018}:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha = \sigma\left(W_{\alpha 2} \cdot \mathrm{GELU}\left(W_{\alpha 1} z_{\text{fused}}\right)\right) \in (0, 1)^d,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  z_{\text{cal}} = z_{\text{fused}} \odot \alpha.
\end{equation}
The calibrated embedding $z_{\text{cal}}$ is fed into a regularized classification head structured with residual layer normalization and progressive dropout \cite{srivastava2014}:
\begin{align}
  h_1 &= \mathrm{Dropout}_{0.25}\left(\mathrm{LayerNorm}\left(W_1 z_{\text{cal}} + b_1\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{256}, \\
  h_2 &= \mathrm{Dropout}_{0.15}\left(\mathrm{LayerNorm}\left(W_2 h_1 + b_2\right)\right) \in \mathbb{R}^{128}, \\
  \hat{y} &= W_3 h_2 + b_3 \in \mathbb{R}^6.
\end{align}

\subsection{Effective-Number Class-Balanced Focal Loss}
To combat the severe 3.76:1 class imbalance between paper (429 samples) and trash (114 samples), we adopt the theory of effective number of samples proposed by Cui et al. \cite{cui2019}. As sample volume $n$ grows, the marginal information gained by adding a new sample diminishes exponentially due to visual feature overlap. The effective volume of data for class $i$ with $n_i$ instances is:
\begin{equation}
  E_{n_i} = \frac{1 - \beta^{n_i}}{1 - \beta},
\end{equation}
where hyperparameter $\beta = 0.999 \in [0, 1)$. The normalized class-balancing weight vector $w \in \mathbb{R}^C$ is defined as:
\begin{equation}
  w_i = \frac{1 - \beta}{1 - \beta^{n_i}} \Bigg/ \sum_{j=1}^C \frac{1 - \beta}{1 - \beta^{n_j}} \times C.
\end{equation}
We combine this weighting scheme with focal modulation ($\gamma = 2.0$) \cite{lin2017} and label smoothing ($\epsilon = 0.04$) \cite{szegedy2016} to prevent overconfident boundary over-fitting:
\begin{equation}
  q_i = y_i (1 - \epsilon) + \frac{\epsilon}{C},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  p_i = \frac{\exp(\hat{y}_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^C \exp(\hat{y}_j)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}_{\text{CB-Focal}} = - \frac{1}{B} \sum_{b=1}^B \sum_{i=1}^C w_i (1 - p_{b, i})^\gamma q_{b, i} \log(p_{b, i}).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Multi-View Test-Time Augmentation and Uncertainty Quantification}
During inference, a test image $x$ undergoes a multi-view transformation suite $\{T_k\}_{k=1}^K$ ($K=10$), encompassing horizontal flips, multi-scale crops ($\pm 5\%$), and subtle affine transformations \cite{shorten2019}. The aggregated posterior probability distribution is computed via ensembling across $M$ independently seeded models ($M=3$) \cite{lakshminarayanan2017, guo2017}:
\begin{equation}
  P(y = c \mid x) = \frac{1}{M \cdot K} \sum_{m=1}^M \sum_{k=1}^K \mathrm{Softmax}\left(f_{\theta_m}(T_k(x))\right)_c.
\end{equation}
To ensure safe deployment in automated sorting bins, we compute the predictive Shannon entropy \cite{shannon1948}:
\begin{equation}
  H(x) = - \sum_{c=1}^C P(y=c \mid x) \log_2 P(y=c \mid x).
\end{equation}
When $H(x) > \tau_{\text{reject}} = 0.35$\,bits, the sample is flagged as an ambiguous outlier and routed to human-in-the-loop review, preventing catastrophic bin contamination.

\subsection{Training Protocol and Implementation Details}
The model was implemented in PyTorch and trained using AdamW optimization \cite{loshchilov2017} with an initial learning rate $\eta = 1.5 \times 10^{-3}$, weight decay $\lambda = 10^{-2}$, and a batch size of 32. We employed a Cosine Annealing learning rate schedule \cite{loshchilov2016} over 45 epochs with minimum learning rate $\eta_{\min} = 10^{-5}$. Training data was augmented with RandomResizedCrop, RandomHorizontalFlip ($p=0.5$), RandomRotation ($\pm 30^\circ$), ColorJitter, and RandomErasing ($p=0.3$) \cite{zhong2020}.

\subsection{Service-Oriented Microservices Architecture}
The EcoSphere AI platform decouples image inference from client interactions through four core microservices:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Inference and Quality Assurance Service:} Exposes an asynchronous REST API endpoint capable of running multi-backbone inference in under 85\,ms per frame on edge/cloud nodes, integrating entropy-based rejection \cite{shannon1948} to flag low-confidence captures.
  \item \textbf{Geolocation and Recycling Routing Service:} Interacts with OpenStreetMap and municipal spatial databases to map nearby authorized recycling centers, tailored to the specific material detected (e.g., electronic waste, clear glass, PET plastics).
  \item \textbf{Community Engagement and Alert Service:} Dispatches push notifications regarding local recycling drives, hazardous material collection schedules, and citizen cleanup initiatives.
  \item \textbf{Real-Time Carbon Telemetry Dashboard:} Converts verified classified waste mass into estimated CO$_2$ equivalent emissions averted, providing gamified community leaderboards and environmental impact reports.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The empirical results corroborate our central hypothesis: high-accuracy waste recognition ($\ge 98\%$) cannot be attained by merely tuning a single CNN backbone, but requires fusing complementary representational paradigms (inverted residuals \cite{tan2019, sandler2018}, additive bottlenecks \cite{he2016}, and dense connections \cite{huang2017}) alongside rigorous class-imbalance compensation \cite{cui2019}.

\paragraph{Threats to Validity:}
While the model attains 98.25\% accuracy on TrashNet, real-world deployment faces environmental variations such as varying solar illumination, dirty optics, and composite multi-material items (e.g., paper coffee cups with polyethylene lining). Our uncertainty gating mechanism ($H(x) > \tau$) \cite{shannon1948, guo2017} mitigates this risk by deferring ambiguous cases to manual operator sorting.

%-------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
\textbf{In this work}, we presented \textbf{EcoSphere AI}, a service-oriented framework for smart environmental sustainability driven by the novel \textbf{EcoWasteNet-CGH} multi-backbone architecture. By dynamically gating complementary features from EfficientNet-B0, ResNet-50, and DenseNet-121, employing Effective-Number Class-Balanced Focal Loss, and executing 10-view Test-Time Augmentation, our system achieves an unprecedented verified state-of-the-art accuracy of \ourfinalacc{} (394/401 correct) on the standardized TrashNet test benchmark. \textbf{The experimental outcome} confirms that our architecture \textbf{outperformed} the 95.00\% benchmark of the base paper by Utku et al. (2026) \cite{utku2026} by +3.25 percentage points while elevating minority trash recall to 95.83\%. Furthermore, the superior \textbf{performance} across all metrics demonstrates the viability of multi-scale gating over single-stream baselines. Coupled with a live cloud service architecture for real-time geolocation, carbon metrics, and community engagement, EcoSphere AI establishes a reproducible, production-ready foundation for modern circular economy systems.
